About

At the intersection of AI and healthcare

I am a computer vision and medical imaging researcher driven by a single conviction: when a physician reads a brain scan, the numbers they see should reflect biology — not the quirks of the scanner that captured it.

During my Ph.D. at Vanderbilt University, I developed physics-informed neural networks that predict and correct scanner-specific distortions directly from imaging data — eliminating errors of 10–30% that had been silently corrupting quantitative brain measurements across multi-center studies for decades. These corrections enabled two landmark downstream studies: the first comprehensive normative white matter brain charts, published in Nature (2026) across 42 cohorts worldwide, and the largest study of white matter asymmetry ever conducted (26,000+ scans, Human Brain Mapping, 2026).

At the U.S. FDA's Center for Devices and Radiological Health, I developed BEHYPE — a framework that detects when AI-based medical devices "think differently" about different patient subgroups, uncovering biases in breast imaging classifiers that affect patients with dense breast tissue. Now at Bioscope AI, I'm building a precision longevity platform that transforms raw brain scans into quantitative health profiles — 95+ structure segmentation, normative contextualization, brain-age estimation, and AI-powered clinical interpretation — pursuing regulatory clearance as a Software as a Medical Device.

Originally from Coimbatore, India, I've studied and worked across three continents. When I'm not building neural networks, you'll find me training for a marathon, on a yoga mat, or exploring a new city. I believe the best science happens when curiosity extends beyond the bench.

Research

Three pillars of impact

My research sits on three interconnected pillars — each addressing a fundamental challenge in getting AI to work safely and equitably in clinical medicine.

Pillar I

Reliable Medical Imaging

Scanner-specific artifacts can introduce 10–30% errors in brain measurements, undermining the multi-center studies that modern neuroscience depends on.

  • DeepN4 — deep learning replacement for the N4ITK bias field correction algorithm, trained on 9,000+ images from 5+ cohorts. Contributed as open-source to the DIPY library, used by thousands of researchers worldwide.
  • Gradient nonlinearity correction — comprehensive suite for modeling and correcting spatially varying gradient field errors in diffusion MRI. 5 first-author papers, 1 provisional US patent.
  • Distortion correction — co-authored methods for correcting susceptibility-induced distortions without reverse phase-encoding (153 citations, most-cited work).

Enabled the first normative white matter brain charts (Nature, 2026) and the largest white matter asymmetry study (HBM, 2026, 26K+ scans)

Pillar II

Fair & Trustworthy AI

AI in healthcare must work equally well for every patient. I build tools that detect and measure when it doesn't.

  • BEHYPE (Bias Evaluation using Hyperdimensional Computing) — model-agnostic framework for detecting bias in AI-based medical devices. Developed at the U.S. FDA's CDRH. Encodes feature representations into hypervectors to detect when a model treats different subgroups differently.
  • LLM reasoning safety — co-authored research on trace-level synthesis in mixture-of-agents systems and measuring causal mechanisms vs. statistical artifacts in LLM probes.
  • Regulatory science — authored internal CDRH review on LLM applications in clinical workflows.

Presented at FDA Scientific Research Day and FDA AI/ML Program. Applied to real breast imaging classifiers.

Pillar III

Clinical AI Translation

Research only matters if it reaches patients. I build the infrastructure that bridges the gap between AI models and clinical care.

  • AI Incubator — designed and deployed a HIPAA-compliant clinical AI platform at Vanderbilt University Medical Center. Containerized 6 AI tools across 7 departments, interfacing with 7.4M+ image series for 246,000 patients.
  • XNAT platform — managed 379+ projects, 48,556+ subjects, 480,000+ scans. Built BIDS export tooling independently replicated at Brown, Oxford, and UCL.
  • Bioscope AI — building a precision longevity platform producing quantitative brain health profiles with 95+ structure segmentation, normative percentile contextualization, brain-age estimation, and disease-pattern selectors for 7 neurodegenerative conditions.

Serving 7 departments across VUMC. BIDS tooling adopted across 78 projects and replicated internationally.
